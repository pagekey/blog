## 1. Something Useful: Open Sourcing Hex

- Open sourced the Hex orchestration engine. It's the heart and brain of the HexBox, now completely free and open to the world.
- A useful tool to automate local processes and your life without relying on cloud services.

## 2. What I Worked On This Week

- Stopped iterating in a cave: surveyed 3 businesses and 2 individuals to find out what problem I'm actually solving.
- Learned that small business owners are drowning trying to be their own IT team.
- Learned that everyday people find cloud apps unfriendly and complex.
- Validated that people want things that "just work" without an IT degree, but they hate lifetime subscriptions.
- Decided on a hard pivot: HexBox Photos Edition.
- Ripped the board out of the old PageKey unit to rebrand it as the Photos Edition prototype.

## 3. What's Next

- Building exactly ONE HexBox Photos Edition to try and sell it. If it sells, I'm onto something. If not, back to talking to people.
- Creating a complete self-hosted photo server installation guide next week.
- Using that guide as the foundation for HexBox Photos.

## 4. Freeform / Ramble

Last week I realized I had built a rock-solid foundation, but I didn't know who it was for. So I stepped out of the code and asked. 

The feedback was clear: small business owners are drowning in IT support, and regular people are tired of complex, unfriendly cloud apps. Everyone wants technology that just works, without being forced into a lifetime subscription.

So, instead of spending months tooling for mass production on an unproven idea, I'm doing the opposite. I'm building exactly one HexBox Photos Edition. I'll put it up for sale. If someone buys it, I have real validation. If not, I know I'm solving the wrong problem again.

The mission hasn't changed. Stop renting your data. We are taking back tech, but this time, making sure it solves a very specific, painful problem.
