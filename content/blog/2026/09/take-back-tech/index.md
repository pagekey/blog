Technology has given us incredible power, but it feels like the more advanced it gets, the easier it is to completely lose track of how it all works. We use devices we don't understand, depend on services we don't control, and increasingly outsource our decisions to systems we can't see.

Recent advances have made just about everything easier and more convenient, but also increasingly centralized. As a society, we face large-scale issues that are hard for a normal person to do anything about. As a result, a lot of people blame the government, big tech, and other huge institutions for the many problems we're facing today.

I'm not here to argue about who's to blame. I'm here with a call to action - regardless of the problems at hand, the responsibility is on you and me to do whatever we can to improve things, for ourselves and for those around us. Don't give your power away to a big organization you have no control over. Instead, Take Back Tech and learn to rely on yourself.

I'm going to run an experiment with my business. I think that, when designed to be transparent and repairable rather than for planned obsolescence and endless profit, technology can empower ordinary people and make them feel, act, and become **more** capable, not less. So let's begin the grand experiment of Take Back Tech and try to answer the question, "How much of our technological agency can we take back?"

## The Idea - Why

Take Back Tech is an idea for giving people agency. It's for creating a world where technology is something we participate in, something we can contribute to and make better, not where it's just something that happens to us.

The goal is not to completely reject modern technology or do everything ourselves. Instead, we need to integrate the full stack into our lives. Rather than being a consumer of a product, become an owner - understand your property, learn, and shape the systems you depend on. Imagine being able to unlock a new feature for an app or device **solely** by your individual efforts, without having to ask anyone to approve it.

My bet is that if more people create and understand the systems that run our daily lives, we'll get a world that's more resilient, creative, transparent, and free.

## The Cycle - How

When you own something, and it's truly yours instead of being stuck with a lifelong subscription, it changes your behavior. I think it could begin a virtuous cycle that looks something like:

> Own -> Understand -> Create -> Share -> Own

Let's break down each piece of the puzzle.

### Own

You start by using your private property to your benefit, instead of relying on someone else's infrastructure. Buy a server, self-host an app, and start using it. Suddenly, you have unlimited access to a service with no paywall, for as long as you want to use it.

### Understand

When you own something, eventually it will need maintenance. Cars need oil, servers need software updates, and broken apps need a sharp mind to dig into what went wrong. We've never had more tools available for fixing software ourselves. The first error you encounter can turn into a rabbit hole that teaches you more about how computers work than any single book could have covered. As a result, you stop treating the thing you own as a black box, something to be consumed, and start to understand how it works under the hood.

### Create

If you use the thing you own enough, and fix enough of these little hiccups, you may find yourself with an idea about how to make the whole thing better. This is where, because you own the technology, you can actually change how things work, tailoring the experience to exactly what you want.

### Share

Great work - you improved something. Now, you can keep it to yourself, or you can share it online so that others can use it too. If you choose to do the latter (remember, it's your choice, since you're the owner), you can have an impact that affects tens, hundreds, thousands, all the way up to the rest of humanity. The sky is the limit. And once you share your work, others can build on it. Which brings us back to...

### Own

With your enthusiasm and commitment to Take Back Tech in your life, you may inspire others to follow in your footsteps, use the lessons you've learned as a head-start, and then go forth on their own journey to make the world better. And thus continues the virtuous cycle of learning.

This all sounds great in theory, but how can we make it real?

## The Experiment - Let's find out.

My hypothesis is that an ordinary person can progressively reclaim technological capability by owning, understanding, creating, and sharing, starting simple and progressing to more and more complex things.

We need a test subject - and it's going to be me. I'm going to go through this process myself, document everything publicly, and see how far I can take it.

I'll start by building Hex, an open-source orchestrator to help anyone host apps and inspect what's going on as deeply as they'd like. I'll bundle this into HexBox and sell it as a ready-to-go package for people who don't have time to start from scratch.

Then, I'll host all the services I rely on in my business, documenting it as I go. Once I've got the software squared away, I'll move onto the physical realm, dabbling in electronics, robotics, and physical products, and finding ways to connect them back to the Hex ecosystem so that they're transparent and easy to understand.

Eventually, I want to understand and own the **entire** stack, all the way down. I'm talking about finding a way to make chips, arguably the most complex and precise process that humanity has ever developed. There must be a way to find an MVP, a simple version of that process I can do myself. But this is a lifetime-scale goal - let's not get too far ahead of ourselves.

## Your Turn - What You Can Do

Feeling excited? Turn that energy into something that helps you and those around you. Here are five things you can do right now to Take Back Tech in your life:

1. **Get curious**: Pick a piece of technology you depend on - an app, your phone, a cloud storage service, your camera - and learn how it works.

2. **Own something**: Start small. Run a server, repair something, install Linux, build an Arduino project, grow some food, or whatever you can think of.

3. **Make something**: Don't just binge tutorials. This isn't Netflix. Use what you're learning to make something of your own, in the real world.

4. **Teach someone else**: Document what you've done, explain it, and help a friend. You'll understand it better by teaching it, and at the same time, you'll be helping someone out.

5. **Lean into your community**: Find people nearby who build things, trade knowledge, help each other, and make your local community more capable.

## Time to Take Back Tech

We don't have to wait for technology to become more open, decentralized, or empowering. We have everything we need to get started and become part of the solution.

Every day, we get to choose between being users and consumers, or leveling up to become participants, builders, and owners.

Technology should make us feel more capable, not less.

I'm not sure how far this experiment can go, but that's the point: we need to find out, and the only way to do it is by taking real action, failing, learning, and trying again.

So go ahead: Take one piece of technology, understand it, build something with it, and share what you learn.

Let's Take Back Tech, together.
